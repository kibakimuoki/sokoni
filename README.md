## The money flow (automatic)
**Buyer pays → provider confirms by signed webhook (plus a polling reconciler) → funds are *held* against the order → seller fulfils →
buyer accepts → Sokoni pays the seller automatically.** Nobody approves a normal payment or payout. Admins only settle **disputes**
(release / refund) and watch the dashboard.
- **Buyers see five methods, never provider names:** 🇰🇪 M-Pesa · 💳 Card · 🅿️ PayPal · 🌍 International payment · ₿ Crypto / stablecoin. Sokoni routes each to a provider
  (`lib/payments.js`): M-Pesa → Daraja (or Flutterwave mobile money); Card → Stripe (or Flutterwave); International → Flutterwave local methods / bank
  transfer / mobile money (or Stripe local methods); Crypto → NOWPayments. Methods with no provider connected are still shown, greyed out as "Not set up yet", so everyone can see the full lineup.
- **Handover codes:** when funds are held, the *seller* gets a 6-digit pickup code and the *buyer* a 6-digit delivery code. A rider/carrier needs the pickup code to
  mark "picked up" and the buyer's delivery code to mark "delivered" — entering it counts as the buyer accepting, which releases payment automatically. Wrong codes are
  rate-limited. Self-pickup orders: buyer taps "I received it", or funds auto-release after `SELF_PICKUP_RELEASE_DAYS` (7); delivered-but-silent orders after `AUTO_RELEASE_DAYS` (3).
- **Safety nets:** idempotent webhooks, amount/currency re-verification (Stripe: signed payload; Flutterwave: server-to-server verify; crypto: HMAC-SHA512 IPN),
  a reconciliation poller for missed webhooks, payout retries with backoff, a provider-event log, and an exceptions list in the console.
- **Payouts** (Account → "Get paid"): sellers choose 🇰🇪 M-Pesa · 🏦 Bank account · 🌍 International bank (SWIFT/IBAN) · 📱 Other mobile money · ₿ Crypto wallet. **M-Pesa and bank payouts are automatic** once M-Pesa B2C or Flutterwave is connected (the bank form uses a built-in directory — Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda, Ethiopia, Nigeria, Ghana, South Africa, Egypt, Zambia, Malawi, India, US, UK — or a live list when Flutterwave is connected, or free-text for any other bank).
  **SWIFT, other mobile money and crypto have no automatic rail yet**: the seller just sees "your payout is being processed", and the payout appears under Operations → Payouts → *Needs manual payment* with the destination details and a "Mark as paid" button. If a seller has no payout method at all, the payout waits and is sent automatically once they add one. Other rails plug in via
  `payouts.registerRoute()` — **Stripe Connect** (needs seller onboarding flows; not available in every country, e.g. Kenya) and **Payoneer** (Mass Payouts API is
  *partner-gated*: you need an approved programme and its docs) are not included. Crypto is collect-only: paying sellers in crypto needs custody/licensing.
- **Crypto:** Coinbase Commerce was shut down on 31 Mar 2026, so crypto uses **NOWPayments** (stablecoins/major coins; priced in USD at checkout). Crypto payments are
  final — refunds are manual (the console flags them). Stripe's own stablecoin checkout can be switched on inside your Stripe account and appears under 💳 Card with no code.
- **Refunds** (dispute outcome): automatic through Stripe or Flutterwave; M-Pesa/crypto are flagged as manual.
- `DEV_PAYMENTS=1` adds a "Test payment (sandbox)" method + test payouts so you can run the whole loop locally. **Never enable it in production.**

## Produce quality reports & trusted listings (Claude vision)
Every listing's 5 live photos go to Claude in one call (`ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`), which returns a **quality report** buyers see on the product page and market card:
**grade (A premium / B good / C basic / reject)**, **freshness %**, **ripeness**, **uniformity**, **defects with severity**, **estimated shelf life**, **best use** (fresh market / processing / export…), a plain-language summary and a **confidence** level.
Buyers can filter the market by grade, and the original report + photos appear in the admin dispute screen as evidence. It is a model's judgement from photos — useful, not a laboratory test — and says so.
- **If the AI is down, selling isn't blocked:** the listing goes live with "quality check pending", Sokoni retries in the background (up to 6 times), and the seller can tap "Run quality check now".
- **Failures are visible:** Operations → Providers shows the model, the last AI call's result and the exact error (wrong key, unknown model, no credit…) and a **Test AI now** button. Set `ANTHROPIC_MODEL` if you want a different model (default `claude-sonnet-5-5`).
- **Produce that looks spoiled or unsafe (grade "reject"), doesn't match the listed product, or screens as high-risk goes to Listing reviews** instead of publishing.
Authenticity signals (not a promise that produce exists): single-use camera session, photos must be taken after it started, exactly 5 distinct photos, SHA-256 + perceptual hashes (exact reuse is blocked; **several** photos (2+) matching another seller's listing hold the listing for review, a single look-alike is only a minor note; server-side hashing needs `sharp`). A flagged seller can **retake the photos** from the app and, if the new ones pass, goes live with no admin involved. While testing with one camera pointed at the same scene or several test accounts, set `PHOTO_NEAR_DUP_DISTANCE=-1` to switch the look-alike check off, GPS at capture vs the listed location (a town-centre pin gets a district-sized tolerance, `PIN_TOWN_RADIUS_KM`, default 60 km; an exact GPS pin is held to 5 km; flagged sellers get a one-tap "use the place where the photos were taken" fix), device metadata, AI screening for screen re-captures / stock / AI-generated / edited images, seller reputation (new → established → trusted), and pickup/delivery handover codes. There is no photo-code challenge.

## Market price guide (live)
"Get a market price guide" asks Claude to **search the web** for current wholesale/farm-gate prices *in the seller's country* and *global/export benchmarks*, compares them with Sokoni's own listings, and returns a suggested range with the real search sources, confidence and date. Web search must be enabled for your Anthropic account; if it isn't, the guide falls back to the model's market knowledge and says "not live". Prices vary by grade/season — it's a guide, not a quote.

## Location & language
- **Typeahead everywhere:** type `ke` → Kenya; type a town and get towns *in the selected country*. Kenya has a built-in offline gazetteer; elsewhere set `GEONAMES_USERNAME`
  (free at geonames.org) or fall back to the keyless Photon geocoder (best effort — use a paid geocoder at scale). "Use my current location" shows what was detected and the
  user confirms or edits it (final authority stays with the user). Products use the same typeahead (`a…` → avocado, apples, aloe vera…) and still accept free text.

  Swahili and French are hand-written; every other language is translated by Claude on first use and cached in `data/i18n/` (needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`). Treat translations as drafts.
  Run `npm run strings` after changing UI text.

## Local delivery: automatic dispatch, live tracking, instant rider pay
- **Buyers choose when ordering** ("Deliver to me" with their GPS location, or "I'll collect it"). Buyers and sellers **never request a rider** — there is no such button. They see the rider's name, **live distance, ETA and map** (updated every few seconds). The buyer's exact coordinates are shared only with the buyer and the rider.
- **The system dispatches.** The moment the buyer's payment is held, it creates the delivery and **offers it to the nearest online, free rider** (within `RIDER_MATCH_RADIUS_KM`). The rider sees the distance and fee and only taps **Accept** or **Decline**. A decline, or no answer within `OFFER_SECONDS` (60), passes the offer to the next nearest rider automatically; if nobody is online it keeps searching and offers the job as soon as a rider comes online or moves into range. Exact pickup coordinates are revealed only after accepting.
- **The delivery fee is part of the order total** (distance × 1.3 road factor, `RIDER_BASE_FEE_USD` + `RIDER_PER_KM_USD`, converted to the order currency — a built-in approximate rate table is used only if live FX is down). It is charged with the order, held with the rest of the money, and paid to the rider.
- **Instant, automatic rider payout:** when the rider enters the buyer's delivery code, the seller **and** the rider are paid automatically (M-Pesa B2C or Flutterwave transfer, to the rider's saved payout method). A rider with no payout method yet is paid **the moment they add one**. Failed transfers retry; the console lists rider payouts with retry / mark-paid.
- **Naming the spot:** "Use my current location" turns the GPS fix into a place name and **types it into the town box** (country too); the user can still edit it. Order of lookup: GeoNames (if `GEONAMES_USERNAME`) → OpenStreetMap Nominatim → Photon → a built-in Kenyan town list (works offline) → the browser asks OpenStreetMap itself. If every lookup fails the box says "My GPS location" and the exact pin is still saved.
- **GPS accuracy:** the app waits (up to ~25 s) for a fix within ~30 m and shows the live "±N m". Phones outdoors usually reach 5–20 m; a laptop estimates from Wi-Fi and can be hundreds of metres off, so the app warns when accuracy is poor and offers "Try again". A browser cannot *guarantee* 30 m.
- **Naming the place:** "Use my location" tries GeoNames, OpenStreetMap Nominatim (set `GEOCODER_EMAIL`, or your own `NOMINATIM_URL`), Photon, and finally the nearest known Kenyan town (marked approximate) — and fills the town box. If nothing can name the spot it says so and keeps the exact position.
- **Messages:** the note a buyer writes with an order request is the first message of that order's conversation, so it appears in the seller's Messages with an unread badge.

## Individuals and companies (cooperatives & farmer groups count as companies)
One account can still **both buy and sell**. At signup traders choose **Individual** or **Company / cooperative** (legal name, business type, registration number, tax ID); an individual can switch to a company later under Account.
- **Verified company badge:** a company uploads its registration certificate (and tax certificate / owner ID) under Account — photos or PDFs, 5 MB max, up to 8 — and submits for review. Documents are stored **privately** (never on the public uploads URL; only an admin can open them) and reviewed in Operations → **Companies**, where an admin approves or rejects with a reason. Changing the legal name, registration number or tax ID removes the badge until it's checked again.
- **Who can buy:** each listing has "Who can buy this?" — everyone, companies & cooperatives only, or verified companies only. This is enforced on the server (hidden from other buyers and blocked at order time), not just in the UI.
- **Who you're dealing with:** listings, orders and the market show the company name, business type and ✓ Verified company badge; buyers can filter the market by seller type (all / companies / verified companies / individuals). Company buyers can add a PO / reference number to an order.
- **Not built yet:** invoices/receipts with tax IDs, team members with roles, higher limits for verified companies.

## Account privacy
Saved payout details are **never sent back to the browser** — the app only receives a masked summary (e.g. `KCB Bank · •••••6789`). Saved sections show as grayed read-only cards with **Edit** (sensitive fields reopen blank) and **Remove**. Adding, changing or removing a payout method requires the account password, so a logged-in session can't redirect payouts.

## M-Pesa, Airtel Money, Equitel and T-Kash
The M-Pesa option now says **"M-Pesa & mobile money"** and shows the four networks as round badges that overlap. The prompt can go to a different phone than the account's (for example an Airtel SIM).

## Keeping the admin console private
`https://your-domain/admin.html` is a public *file* - anyone can open it and see the sign-in box - but it contains no data and no secrets, and **every admin action needs your admin password** (ADMIN_TOKEN). Because it is reachable from the internet, protect it in layers:
1. **A long random ADMIN_TOKEN** (24+ characters, different from SERVER_SECRET). To make one: `node -e "console.log(require('crypto').randomBytes(24).toString('base64url'))"`. The server warns at start-up if it is short.
2. **Wrong guesses are limited.** 8 wrong passwords from one address block that address for 15 minutes (`ADMIN_MAX_FAILS`, `ADMIN_LOCK_MINUTES`). Asking with no password never counts.
3. **A secret address (recommended):** `ADMIN_PATH=/ops-<your-own-random-name>.html` moves the console there and `/admin.html` then says "Not found". **Make your own name** - never copy an example from a document (anyone who has read it knows it). This command prints a ready-to-paste line: `node -e "console.log('ADMIN_PATH=/ops-' + require('crypto').randomBytes(8).toString('hex') + '.html')"`. Put it in your settings file, restart, and the start-up message shows the new address. **Not sure it worked? Run `node tools/check-setup.js`** in the app folder: it reads your settings file the way the server does and tells you which file was read, whether any file is from a different download, the exact address of your admin console (or why the setting isn't active), and whether your admin password looks safe. (If you see the normal Sokoni page at your admin address, the setting isn't active: a wrong or unknown address always shows the normal page, on purpose.) It hides the *page*; the password, the wrong-guess limit and (best) `ADMIN_ALLOWED_IPS` are what actually protect the console. Treat the address like a password: don't share screenshots of the address bar.
4. **Only your own addresses (optional, strongest):** `ADMIN_ALLOWED_IPS=1.2.3.4,5.6.7.8` makes the page and every admin request answer "Not found" to everyone else. (Home internet addresses change - keep a way to edit the setting.)
5. **Behind a proxy**, tell Sokoni so, so the real visitor address is used for 2 and 4: `TRUST_PROXY=1` when you sit behind Caddy or nginx (as in DEPLOY.md), or `TRUST_PROXY=cloudflare` behind Cloudflare **only if your server accepts traffic from nowhere else** (otherwise that header can be faked). Don't set it at all if visitors reach the server directly.
6. **HTTPS only**, so the password never travels in the clear. The page also tells search engines not to index it.

## Your domain (sokony.com)
The app's name stays **Sokoni**; only the web address is sokony.com. Set `PUBLIC_BASE_URL=https://www.sokony.com` (or `https://sokony.com` - pick one and make the other redirect to it). Then: (1) re-register the callback addresses that were on the temporary `trycloudflare.com` address - the Equity payment and payout callbacks (shown in Operations -> Payment setup -> Equity) and, for any other provider you use, its webhook; NOWPayments gets its address with every payment, so nothing to register there; (2) verify `sokony.com` with your e-mail provider (add the DNS records it shows) and send from an address like `Sokoni <no-reply@sokony.com>`; (3) add `PUSH_SUBJECT=mailto:you@sokony.com`; (4) if you publish an Android app, its asset-links file is served from the same domain. Keep the data folder permanent when you move hosts.

## Ratings and feedback
After an order is received (the buyer confirms, or it is released automatically), the **buyer** is offered a rating of the farmer: 1-5 stars, *did it match the photos and the quality report?*, *was it on time?* and an optional comment (phone numbers in comments are hidden). One rating per order; the buyer can change it for 7 days. A farmer's average (4.7 stars and the number of ratings) shows on their listings, **"See feedback"** shows the star bars, how many said it matched the photos and were on time, and the comments - never who wrote them - and well-rated farmers rank slightly higher in the market (from 3 ratings; at most about a day and a half either way). Farmers see their own feedback under **Account -> Your ratings as a seller** and are notified of each new rating.
**Reminders:** a buyer who hasn't rated is reminded every **2 days** (push, and e-mail once an e-mail provider is connected) - one message per buyer even if several orders are waiting - up to 5 times per order, only for orders received in the last 14 days; **Not now** stops reminders for that order. The Orders page also shows a "waiting for your rating" banner. *Operations -> Ratings* lists every rating and lets you hide an abusive one (kept on record, can be restored). The buyer's answer to "did it match the photos?" is also saved as real-world proof for the quality labels in the training dataset.

## Analytics
**Operations -> Analytics** is the company's picture of the whole marketplace: sales, average order, kilograms moved, completion/refund/dispute rates, repeat buyers, commission; **what sells** (by product and category, price per kilo, average quality, grade A share, ratings, live animals); **where it comes from** (farmer's town and country) and **where it goes** (buyer country, routes, cross-border share); **quality** (the mix of grades offered, the best-quality crops, and whether the grade tells the truth - the dispute and refund rate for each grade); top sellers and buyers; the ratings picture; how buyers pay; delivery; the order funnel; supply and sell-through; monthly sales. Filter by 7 days / 30 days / 90 days / a year / all time, by farmer country and by category.
**It is stored permanently**, not just shown: one row per order, one per listing and one per day are kept in your data folder (so the automatic backups cover them), rebuilt every 10 minutes, and **never deleted** - even if an order or listing is later removed. Download them as CSV from the Analytics tab, or run `node tools/export-analytics.js C:\sokoni-analytics-export` to write them to a folder. The files show people as one-way keys, never names, phones or e-mails.

## Units: weight, count and volume
Each product offers the units that make sense for it. **Solids** are sold by weight or count (kilogram, tonne, 50 kg and 90 kg bags, crates, boxes, bunches, pallets, container loads). **Liquids** - milk, fermented milk (mala), yoghurt, cooking and palm oil, coconut and avocado oil, juice, coconut water, honey - are sold by **volume**: litre, 20-litre jerrycan, 200-litre drum or 1,000-litre tank (honey and oils can also be sold by weight). **Live animals** are sold per head and eggs by the tray or dozen. Picking a product, from the quick chips or from the search box, sets the right unit by itself and hides the units that don't fit (no litres of tomatoes, no sacks of milk); the server refuses a mismatched listing too. A product you type in yourself can use any unit. For delivery, a litre is weighed by what it is (milk about 1.03 kg, oil 0.92, honey 1.42, plus the container), so a 200-litre drum of oil is a tuk-tuk job and a pair of 1,000-litre tanks goes to freight quotes.

## Animals
Livestock (cattle, goats, sheep, pigs, poultry, rabbits, camels, donkeys…), animal products (eggs, milk, meat, hides, wool) and fish are in the product list. Live animals are sold **per head**; the seller must say whether they have the **ownership and movement papers** (shown to buyers), the photo check judges **body condition** instead of freshness (and never claims a vet's verdict), delivery is priced by the animals' weight and only riders with a suitable vehicle are offered the job (goats need at least a tuk-tuk, cattle a pickup; a very heavy load goes to freight quotes), and international shipments ask for a veterinary certificate and the movement permit.

## The storefront and ordering
- **A public front page.** Visitors who are not signed in land on the storefront, not the sign-in page: the app download card, a welcome banner ("Create a free account" / "Sign in"), promo cards, the products, categories, "How Sokoni works" and a "Grow or trade produce?" invitation, with the same search and category links as members. Clicking a product shows it (photos, price, seller, rating, quality report) with a "Sign in or create a free account to order" prompt right under the price; after signing in they land straight back on that product. Visitors see only listings meant for everyone, and never a seller's phone, email, payout details or exact map position (tested). The sign-in page is one click away (header, ☰ menu, product pop-up), and signing out lands on it. To open the sign-in page directly use `/#signin` (or `/#signup`).
- **Amazon-style interface.** A dark header with a wide search bar with a category picker, Account / Orders / Messages, a green category strip, and a ☰ side menu listing all categories. The Market home is promo cards, then category cards, then the listings. Searching or choosing a category shows results with a count and "Clear all filters". On phones the header is one line (logo, app button, language, Account) plus a full-width search bar. The Market / Sell / Orders / Messages / Account tabs stay at the bottom on every screen size. The promo cards are compact, and a listing picture that fails to load is replaced by the product's emoji. The glowing card borders are now an option in Account (off by default).
- **Ordering.** Sending an order first shows a review with the exact total and a Back button. Until it is paid a buyer can **edit** it (quantity, note, delivery, PO number) or **cancel** it; if the seller had accepted and the price changes, it goes back to them to accept again. A PIN prompt that may still be live blocks cancelling for 3 minutes, then the payment provider is asked whether it was paid. A payment that arrives for an already-cancelled order is **refunded automatically**. Cancelled or declined orders can be removed from your list (the record is kept).
- **Payment fee.** The payment provider's own fee (1.5% on M-Pesa and Airtel Money) is added to what the buyer pays and shown as a "payment fee" line, so the order's money reaches you in full (switchable in Payment setup).
- **Payment networks** that are not offered (Equitel, T-Kash through Paystack) are not shown at all.

## Payments: Paystack
The chosen route for Kenya: **M-Pesa and Airtel Money PIN prompts and card payments** into your Paystack balance (held there with manual payouts until delivery is confirmed), then automatic M-Pesa payouts to sellers and riders and refunds out of the balance (Operations → Payment setup → Paystack). The card form opens **over Sokoni — buyers never leave the page** — and card numbers never reach Sokoni (a Paystack redirect page is the automatic fallback). While Paystack is connected **buyers see Paystack's options (M-Pesa, Airtel Money, Card) plus crypto**; Equity appears once its codes are entered, and one setting (*Payment options buyers see*) shows PayPal, Stripe, Flutterwave and anything else again. Notifications are signed and double-checked with Paystack, a payout is never sent twice, a short balance makes a payout wait, and with a live key automatic payouts stay off until you switch them on. **Real customers only after Paystack's status is Approved and its written confirmation of the hold-until-delivery use** — see PAYMENTS-SETUP.md 4k and GO-LIVE.md.

## Updating Sokoni without losing anything
Each new zip unpacks into a **new, empty folder**. Your settings (`.env`: bank details, API keys) and your data (the `data` folder: users, listings, orders, saved keys) live in the folder you used before, so a new version starts blank — your payment options and bank details seem to "disappear". The app prints exactly which settings file and data folder it is using every time it starts. Two ways to never lose them:
1. **Copy** `.env` and the `data` folder from the old version into the new one before starting it; or
2. **Keep them in one permanent place:** save your settings as `sokoni.env` in the folder that *contains* the `sokoni-app` folder (it is found automatically by every version), and set `SOKONI_DATA_DIR` in that file to a permanent folder (for example `C:\\sokoni-data`).

## Country and language detection
The app decides where a visitor is in this order: **their own choice → their account → the device's time zone → the internet connection (CDN header) → browser language**. Only the time zone and the connection can switch the app's language automatically, the browser language is only a weak hint, and an automatic guess is re-checked on every visit (a language the person picks themselves always sticks). The flag in the header shows the country in use; tapping it opens the country/language chooser. When a seller presses "Use my current location", a rough internet-based position (worse than ±5 km) is refused, and a position in a different country from their account or time zone is questioned (VPN or proxy?) before the listing's country or currency can change.

## Accounts and sign-in codes
Sign-in codes (SMS + email, with 2FA at every login) **switch on by themselves** the moment you have connected **both** an SMS provider and an email provider **and** sent yourself a successful test through each (Operations → Payment setup → *Send a test … to me*). Until then, sign-up and login need only a phone number, an email and a strong password — and nobody's number or email is verified, so read the risks in PAYMENTS-SETUP.md section 2b. Once they switch on, everyone who signed up without codes is asked to confirm their phone and email at their next login. There is no setting to flip. (`VERIFY_CODES=1` forces codes on, for local testing with `DEV_OTP`; `VERIFY_CODES=0` forces them off.) The test step is a safety catch: a mistyped key would otherwise lock every user out. Admins can reset a forgotten password under **Operations → Users**. **Receipts** are e-mailed through the e-mail provider the moment it is connected.

## Equity Bank (Jenga)
The M-Pesa PIN prompt can be sent through Equity Bank's Jenga API, with the money settled in real time into your Equity account and the order held automatically when Equity calls back (setup in PAYMENTS-SETUP.md section 4f; Operations → Payment setup has the form and a one-click signing-key generator). Payouts through Equity are off until you switch them on after a small live test.

## Payment policy
Buyers in Kenya pay with **M-Pesa Paybill** (a prompt on the phone, or from their own M-Pesa menu — confirmed automatically); everyone else, and Kenyan orders above M-Pesa's limit, pay in **USDC**. Every payment in and out is recorded by the company: users see it under **Account → Payments & earnings** (with a CSV statement), a receipt is emailed to the address they signed up with, and riders are paid the instant the delivery code is confirmed. **Sokoni Coin** is an in-app reward that lowers sellers' fees. See PAYMENTS-SETUP.md sections 4a–4c.

## Paying from anywhere
Buyers are charged in a currency their chosen method supports (their own, else USD) with the exact amount shown first and re-checked on every confirmation; a small buffer (`FX_MARGIN_PERCENT`, default 2) protects you from exchange-rate moves. PayPal is supported alongside cards, M-Pesa, bank transfer and crypto, and **Operations → Payment setup** shows a live coverage map for **all 200 countries** (7 world regions, plus a look-up box for any country: what works, what currency, what isn't reachable yet). Cuba, Iran, North Korea and Syria are blocked by default (providers refuse them); mobile money appears for buyers in the African countries Flutterwave covers.

## Going live with real payments
**Operations → Payment setup** is where you connect everything: five tiles show whether **M-Pesa, Card, PayPal, International payment (secure page and bank transfer / wire / ACH) and Crypto** are ready to receive money, with forms for each provider's keys (stored encrypted, shown masked), the addresses to register, and Test connection. A buyer abroad can pay by card, by bank transfer to your USD account (held once it arrives; confirmed automatically by a signed notice or with one click), or by crypto.
**Read [PAYMENTS-SETUP.md](PAYMENTS-SETUP.md)** (includes which countries can pay you and what isn't reachable yet, e.g. Pix/boleto and Alipay/WeChat) — which providers a Kenyan business can use (Flutterwave first; Stripe isn't available directly), what to register, how to deploy, webhook addresses, the soft-launch plan and a runbook. In the app, **Operations → Providers** shows a live go-live checklist, the exact addresses to register, and a **Test connection** button per provider; **Payments → Export CSV** gives your accountant a reconciliation file.
Safety already built in: atomic data writes with automatic backups (and refusal to overwrite a corrupt file), login throttling, `/api/health`, an optional launch cap (`MAX_ORDER_USD`), the fake test payments that switch themselves off on a public address, M-Pesa Paybill **and** Till support, and payouts that wait for provider settlement instead of failing. `npm test` ignores your `.env` and shell settings so results are the same on every machine.

## Before real money moves
1. **Holding customer funds is regulated** (payment-institution / escrow licensing, KYC/AML, sanctions). Prefer licensed providers' hold-and-settle features and take legal advice.
2. **Everything provider-facing was tested against stubs only** (request shapes, signatures, flows) — run Stripe, Flutterwave, NOWPayments and Daraja in their sandboxes first.
3. Agreements are starter text. 4. No security audit; payout details are plain JSON in `data/` — encrypt at rest, restrict access, back up. 5. Open to every country by default;
`BLOCKED_COUNTRIES` is for legal restrictions. 6. Marketplaces need density in a corridor — launch broadly, concentrate on one or two real trade lanes.

## Sign in with Google or Facebook, search engines, and the look of the app
- **Continue with Google / Facebook.** Buttons appear on the log-in and sign-up pages as soon as the two keys of a provider are saved (Operations → Payment setup → “Google and Facebook sign-in”). Redirect addresses to register: `https://YOUR-SITE/api/auth/google/callback` and `https://YOUR-SITE/api/auth/facebook/callback`. The server checks a signed state and a cookie on the way back, hands the browser a one-time code (never the session token), and links to an existing account only when that account's email was already verified. People who sign up this way have no phone number until they add one; riders and freight carriers still sign up with phone and email.
- **Search engines.** `/sitemap.xml` (home page plus every public, available listing as `/?listing=ID`), `/robots.txt` (hides `/api/`, uploads and the admin page), and the home page carries a description, canonical link, Open Graph/Twitter card and JSON-LD naming Sokoni with its TikTok and Facebook pages. Submit `https://YOUR-SITE/sitemap.xml` in Google Search Console.
- **Look.** The animated lights are on unless a person switches them off in Account; every page has its own colour theme; the Sokoni mark always turns, other icons turn once when pressed, and navigation squashes like jelly when tapped. Devices set to “reduce motion” get none of it.
